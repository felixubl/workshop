Odometer

A row of reels. Each one shows a digit from 0 up to one less than the base. When a reel passes its last digit it goes back to 0 and pushes the reel on its left up by one.

0 to 255

= 0 in base 10

Place values

ran off the last reel, back to 0

A reel with b digits has to turn b times to come back to 0. So one full turn of a reel is one step of the reel on its left, and each reel is worth b times the one on its right: 1, 2, 4, 8 in base 2, and 1, 10, 100 in base 10.

The reels are geared to each other, and differently on each side. On its right edge a reel has one tooth per digit. On its left edge it has a single red tooth, where it passes from its last digit back to 0. A reel turns freely until that tooth comes round; then the tooth catches the tooth of the reel on its left and pushes it up one. A reel showing its last digit has its red tooth waiting just under its neighbour's, so a run of them tips over one after another, each a moment after the one that pushes it.

The teeth are drawn only around the reading line. Anywhere else, a red tooth moving one digit would run into a tooth of its neighbour's full row, which is why a real counter has a small transfer wheel behind the frame at exactly this one spot, doing the pushing.
